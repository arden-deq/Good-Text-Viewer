The lantern at the end of the pier was still burning when Nora
reached the harbor. She had expected the tide to be out by then,
but dark water lapped at the steps and carried the smell of rain
into the empty street. The wind had rattled the shutters all
afternoon, and the merchant who usually swept the front steps
had locked his shop before the first drops fell.

Earlier that evening, her brother had left a note beneath the
blue teapot. He promised to be home before the bells rang, and
he had drawn a little boat in the margin where his name should
have been. A line of damp sand led from the front gate to the
kitchen, where the kettle stood cold beside two cups with their
handles turned toward the window.

Across the water, the windows of the old ferry office glowed
faintly behind the fog. Nora could make out the shape of a chair
and the pale rectangle of a map that nobody had bothered to take
down. The ferry had stopped running three winters ago, after the
council sold the landing to a man who never came to look at what
he had bought.

One by one the houses on the hill went dark, until all she could
see was the light between the boards of a fishing shed. A shadow
moved behind it and then settled back into the quiet. None of
the neighbors had come out to watch the storm, although the
first autumn gale usually drew a crowd to the stone wall above
the harbor.

She stepped down to the lowest stair and listened for the oars
she remembered from summer. Somewhere in the dark a rope scraped
against wood, and the sound was so ordinary that she almost
laughed. The water had crept high enough to reach a brass ring
set into the stone, and in its reflection she could just see the
lantern swinging above her.

Nora folded the note and slipped it into the pocket of her coat.
She decided to wait until the lantern went out, since the harbor
seemed to be the only place left where a promise might still
find her. Beyond the breakwater a small green light appeared
where the channel curved, then vanished for a moment as the boat
passed behind the weathered post.
